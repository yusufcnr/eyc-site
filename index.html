Mobility guide

Mobility usually changes gradually, so early signs are easy to miss. These are general pointers for owners, not veterinary advice; if you notice changes in how your dog moves, talk to your vet first.

Early signs to watch for

  • Hesitating at stairs or before jumping into the car
  • Slow or effortful standing up after rest
  • Back legs slipping on smooth floors
  • Stiffness that eases after moving around
  • Lagging behind or shorter walks

Small changes at home

  • Rugs or runners on slippery floors
  • A supportive bed that is easy to step into
  • Food and water bowls within easy reach
  • Limiting unsupervised access to stairs

Helping your dog safely

  • Support both the chest and the hips, not just the belly
  • Lift with your legs and keep your back straight
  • Let your dog do as much of the work as they can
  • Move slowly and keep sessions short

Keeping them active

  • Shorter, more frequent walks on even ground
  • Keeping a healthy weight to reduce joint load
  • Gentle, vet-approved exercise or physiotherapy
  • Regular check-ups as your dog gets older
